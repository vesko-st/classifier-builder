# Tweet hate labels: how the annotators actually apply the policy

## The pool
The pool has 1,000 records: 42% hate and 58% not_hate. Nearly every record falls into one of two kinds:
- **Immigration tweets.** These are mostly US border and deportation hashtags, plus some European refugee content.
- **Tweets using the gendered slurs** "bitch", "hoe", "whore" or "cunt". These make up about half the pool.

Almost nothing else appears. I found no tweets about women that use no slur at all (kitchen jokes, anti-feminist arguments). I also found no material targeting other groups that doesn't also involve immigrants or one of these slurs. The labels can't tell you how such messages should be treated.

## Immigrants: what counts as hate
**Rule as applied (fairly confident):** a tweet is hate when its body says something hostile or degrading about immigrants as people. That covers:
- crime stories framed as typical of immigrants ("another illegal alien rapes a child… #DeportThemAll")
- claims that they are invaders, animals, parasites, scum or "3rd world" people
- claims that they drain welfare, spread disease or steal jobs
- calls to deport them, remove them or shoot them

Ironic jabs also count, such as "another wonderful dreamer in the news". The slur "rapefugee" used approvingly is usually hate. Tweets about Muslims or Islam count as hate when tied to migrants (for example, ban Islam and send the migrants home). The annotators apply the target group widely, covering Mexicans, Hispanics, "mestizos", Africans and Somalis framed as migrants.

**Consistency:** tweets with explicit dehumanising words or calls for violence are labelled hate about nine times in ten. Crime-report tweets with deportation hashtags are hate about four times in five.

## Immigrants: what stays not_hate
**Policy talk and wall support.** Plain support for a wall, border security, ending DACA or enforcing the law is mostly not_hate. Some examples: "get it built", "fund the wall", donation drives, "they should apply legally like legal immigrants". About nine in ten pure wall-support tweets are not_hate. A minority with no denigrating content still got hate, such as "IT NEVER ENDS! #BuildTheWall" or "use the Army to build the wall".

**Hashtags alone don't decide the label.** This is where the labels depart from the policy's statement that approving hashtag campaigns are typically hateful.
- #BuildThatWall / #BuildTheWall: only about a third of the 230 tweets using them are hate.
- #DeportThemAll: about three in four are hate.
- #SendThemBack: about two in three are hate.

What decides is the body of the tweet. A common not_hate pattern is a body that attacks Democrats, Congress, a named politician or "liberals", with immigration hashtags tacked on the end. These are labelled not_hate about nine times in ten, even with #DeportThemAll. Hashtag-spam tweets ("please follow back #MAGA #BuildThatWall") are not_hate. So are off-topic uses, such as a hashtag on an office refurbishment.

However, a very short approving tweet that consists mainly of deportation hashtags is usually hate ("Well stated! #BuildThatWall #DeportThemAll").

**Reporting, criticism and neutral news.** Tweets that criticise anti-immigrant hashtags are not_hate, as are tweets that debunk rapefugee statistics or mock the term. Neutral news headlines about migrants are not_hate. A crime headline with only a wall hashtag and no comment is split: sometimes hate, sometimes not.

**Known noise.** Two near-identical tweets in the pool got opposite labels. Expect roughly 15–20% disagreement on borderline immigration tweets.

## Women: what counts as hate
**Rule as applied (moderately confident):** a tweet is hate when gendered slurs are aimed in hostility at a woman or at women. This covers:
- an argument or callout where the target gets "bitch", "hoe", "whore" or "cunt"
- threats to fight, slap, drag or "beat that bitch"
- sexualised degradation ("you was riding that dick, hoe")
- generalisations about women, such as "bitches be…" or "these hoes ain't…"
- wishes for rape ("hope this bitch gets raped")

Several slurs stacked in one hostile line ("bitch… you dumb hoe") is the strongest signal. Such tweets are hate about three times in four. Insults with "ugly", "dumb", "shut up" or "fuck you" plus a slur aimed at a woman are hate about three times in four. Sexual content about women is hate about four times in five.

**Where the labels are looser than the policy:**
- The policy says hate means targeting someone *because* she is a woman. The annotators don't check motive: a slur-laden insult in an ordinary personal dispute counts.
- Women insulting other women in AAVE trash talk is often labelled hate.
- Even fairly playful lines that pair "bitch" and "hoe" often get hate ("yes bitch you better tag me back hoe").
- Rap lyrics quoted with "these hoes" got hate.

## Women: what stays not_hate
These categories are fairly reliable, at roughly eight to nine in ten not_hate:
- **Affectionate or friendly use:** birthday wishes, "love you, hoe", "my best friend, bitch".
- **Talking about yourself:** "I'm a whole new bitch", "I'm a basic bitch".
- **Exclamations:** "bitch, I want those shoes", "BITCH he looks so good".
- **Idioms:** "karma's a bitch", "life's a bitch", "son of a bitch", "celiac's a bitch".
- **Reporting or condemning misogyny:** "I got called a slut and a whore", "stop calling girls whores", "if he calls you a bitch he's sexist".

**Male targets are not_hate** (fairly confident, about three in four). This includes "bitch boy", "Trump's bitch", a senator called a whore, a named man called a cunt, and "hoe ass nigga". Insults at female politicians that use no slur are also not_hate.

## Where the women labels are noisy
This is the least consistent part of the data. Many tweets that look like clear abuse of a woman are still labelled not_hate:
- an explicit sexual claim on a woman ("your pussy belongs to me")
- "you hoe ass bitch… hypocrite"
- "nasty bitch" aimed at a named woman

A single "bitch" used as a hostile address ("you dumb bitch", "are those not grapes bitch") is close to a coin flip. A spam porn title stuffed with slurs was not_hate. Overall, about 43% of the slur tweets are hate. For hostile slur tweets aimed at a woman, I'd estimate the labels agree with the rule above only about 70% of the time. Expect a ceiling on achievable accuracy here.

## Other groups and mixed cases
- Hostility toward groups outside the two targets is not_hate when it stands alone: Muslims with no migrant angle, gay people, Jews, Democrats, "liberals". This matches the policy, but there are few examples.
- A tweet that slurs another group *and* migrants is hate because of the migrant part.
- A tweet that is hateful toward women and also uses another slur ("tranny lookin hoes", "retarded cunt bitch") is hate.
- General profanity with no target from the two groups is not_hate.

## Summary of departures from the written policy
1. **Hashtags:** approving campaign hashtags don't by themselves make a tweet hate. Wall hashtags alone are mostly not_hate. A political-attack body with deportation hashtags is mostly not_hate.
2. **Women:** the labels don't require misogynistic motive. Slur-heavy hostility at a woman in an ordinary personal fight counts as hate, including woman-on-woman trash talk.
3. **Inconsistency:** the women's slur category, especially single-slur or semi-playful insults, is labelled unreliably. Borderline immigration tweets have roughly 15–20% disagreement.
4. **Gaps:** the policy's non-slur misogyny, and hate against other groups that doesn't overlap with the two targets, are almost absent from the pool. The labels give no guidance for them.
